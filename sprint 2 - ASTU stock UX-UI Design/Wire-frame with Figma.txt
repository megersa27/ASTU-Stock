1. What is a Wireframe?
A wireframe is a simple visual blueprint of a screen.
Think of it like the architectural plan of a building.
Before constructing a house, an architect doesn't start by choosing curtains.
First:
Where is the entrance?
Where are the rooms?
Where are the doors?
Where are the windows?
Similarly, before designing the final UI:
Where is the sidebar?
Where is the search?
Where is the table?
Where is the Add button?
Where are the filters?
Where are the actions?
That's wireframing.

3. Why Do We Need Wireframes?
Suppose we immediately create a beautiful dashboard:
🎨 Colors
✨ Gradients
 Icons
 Shadows
Then we discover:
"The Store Keeper actually needs quick access to Receive Stock."
We have to redesign everything.
Wireframes allow us to discover problems cheaply.
Wireframe:
5 minutes to change
Final UI:
30+ minutes to change
Developed application:
Much more expensive to change
Therefore:
Find design problems before coding.
That's the main purpose.

10. Why is a Shared Layout Important?
Imagine every page has completely different navigation.
Bad:
Dashboard → sidebar left

Inventory → menu at top

Reports → menu right

Suppliers → no navigation
Very confusing.
Instead:
┌─────────┬────────────────┐
│ Sidebar │ Main Content   │
└─────────┴────────────────┘
is consistent.
Later in React, this can become something like:
AppLayout
├── Sidebar
├── Header
└── MainContent

Wireframe
   ↓
UI Component
   ↓
React Component
We are already thinking about development without writing code yet.

27. Don't Use AI to Design Everything
You can use AI as a design reviewer.
For example, after creating your Dashboard in Figma, ask:
"Act as a UX designer. Review this ASTU Stock Management dashboard. Identify usability problems, missing information, confusing navigation, accessibility issues, and unnecessary elements. Do not redesign it; only give me constructive feedback."
That's a much better learning exercise than:
"AI, design my whole dashboard."
Because you want to learn how designers think.
